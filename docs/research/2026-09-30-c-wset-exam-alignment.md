# Research track C — Aligning the game with WSET Level 3 and the Level 4 Diploma

Part of the 2026-09-30 fun-and-learning research (synthesis: `2026-09-30-fun-and-learning.md`). Web research by a research
agent on public sources; all URLs returned HTTP 200 on 2026-09-30. Everything is paraphrased; community and blog claims are
marked as such. IP note in §8.

## 1. Level 3: what is assessed
Specification Issue 2 (2022); 84 hours total (≈ 30 guided, ≈ 51.5 private).

- **Unit 1 theory.**
  - **LO1:** vineyard and winery factors → style, quality, price. Ranges:
    - vine, growth cycle and grape components
    - climate classes and site factors; weather and vintage variation; soils
    - training, pruning, canopy, density, harvest timing and method
    - hazards; pests, diseases and phylloxera/rootstocks; sugar concentration (noble rot, drying, freezing)
    - organic and biodynamic growing
    - O₂, SO₂, vessels, alcoholic fermentation, MLC, must adjustments
    - white, red and rosé methods, including whole-bunch and carbonic maceration; sweet-wine methods
    - oak and inert maturation, lees, oak alternatives, blending, clarification, stabilisation, closures
  - **LO2:** still wines of about 150 named areas in 17 countries: grapes, law and commerce.
  - **LO3:** sparkling wine and its methods.
  - **LO4:** fortified wine: Port, Sherry, fortified Muscats.
  - **LO5:** recommendations, storage and service, faults, food pairing, social and health issues.
- **Unit 2 tasting:** describe principal still wines with the L3 Systematic Approach; assess quality and readiness.

| Part | Format | Weighting | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theory 1 | 50 MCQ | LO1 8 · LO2 28 · LO3 5 · LO4 5 · LO5 4 | 55 % |
| Theory 2 | 4 short written answers × 25 marks (2 h total with the MCQs) | LO2 70 · LO3+4 20 · LO5 10; LO1 runs through all | 55 % separately |
| Tasting | 2 blind still wines in 30 min | white 20 marks, red 21 (tannin) | 55 % |

Grades: Distinction ≥ 80 % with no paper < 65 %; Merit 65–79 %; Pass 55–64 %. **LO2 carries 56 % of the MCQ marks and 70 %
of the written marks**, so regional breadth, with the factors applied to each region, is the biggest block of marks.

**Question styles** (official sample paper): methods ("name and describe two rosé methods"), "explain why Pinot Noir suits
cool climates", natural factors that moderate a region's climate, why a producer uses MLF, label-based "how was each made",
storage and decanting. Parts are small (2–7 marks); the command verbs are name, identify, describe, explain, state.

**What is rewarded.** "Explain" means linking factor → mechanism → effect → style or quality. Community guides; the L3 book
is written as cause and effect. Listing causes without links earns only the identify marks. "Not answering the question
set" leads the examiner themes (WSET Diploma guidance).

**Tasting marking.** At L3 only the 20/21 split is official. From Diploma guidance and blogs:
- name every aroma type present
- "simple" earns credit on a simple wine
- a range ("medium to medium(+)") or a non-SAT word scores zero

Candidates taste about 40 wines on the course; strong candidates report around 100 (anecdotal).

## 2. The systematic approach compared with the game's sheet
Only the structure is summarised here.
- **Appearance:** 3-step intensity; colours by type, each ending in brown; other observations (legs, deposit, pétillance).
- **Nose:** 5-step intensity; primary, secondary and tertiary characteristics; development (youthful → tired).
- **Palate:** 6-step sweetness; 5-step acidity, tannin, body, flavour intensity and finish; 3-step alcohol (separate bands
  for fortified); mousse; flavour characteristics.
- **Conclusions:** 6-level quality and 4-option readiness (too young / can drink, has potential / drink now, not suitable
  for ageing / too old).
- Tannin nature is a Diploma line, not L3.

Quality: counting balance, length, intensity and complexity (BLIC, 0–4 → poor–outstanding) is a **community heuristic, not
WSET policy**. WSET's Diploma guidance judges quality holistically on seven indicators and asks for positive and negative
evidence plus why the wine is not in the level above.

| Line | Game today | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Scales | Match in code | `docs/science/tasting-sheet.md` still describes 3/4-step scales (doc drift) |
| Alcohol bands | < 11 / 11–13.9 / ≥ 14 % | Confirmed by WSET Diploma Tasting Guidance (2023): the "reviewer to confirm" flag can cite it. Fortified bands 15–16.4 / 16.5–18.4 / ≥ 18.5 % |
| Colour | Whites stop at amber, reds at tawny | Brown missing; no rosé colours (no rosé yet) |
| Other observations | None | Deposit, pétillance, legs |
| Aromas | Families, marked by overlap | Exams mark specific descriptors across clusters; the white "fruit" family merges green, citrus, stone and tropical; no credit for "simple"; no fresh/cooked or ripeness qualifiers; no pepper family for reds although rotundone is modelled |
| Flavour characteristics | Same as nose | Asked separately in the systematic approach |
| Tannin nature | Yes | Ahead of L3; exactly what the Diploma asks for |
| Quality | Count of four checks | Mechanical; causes the high skew; no positive/negative argument or "why not higher" |
| Readiness | 3 options | "Too young" and "too old" missing; "not for sale as it is" is not an option in the systematic approach |

## 3. Diploma (Level 4)
Specification Issue 1.4 (Aug 2025); 500 hours; L3 is required, D1 first.

Units:
- D1 Production 20 %
- D2 Business 10 %
- D3 Wines of the World 50 %: theory, plus 12 wines blind in two 90-minute papers; both halves need 55 %
- D4 Sparkling 5 %
- D5 Fortified 5 %
- D6 research 10 %

Unit grades: 75/65/55 %.

**D1 depth beyond L3:**
- aroma precursors, evapotranspiration, soil pH
- climate change, fire and smoke taint, nematodes and moths, precision viticulture
- mass vs clonal selection, vine age
- flash détente, thermovinification, hyperoxidation, flotation, micro-oxygenation, protein stability
- quality control and HACCP

Verbs rise to evaluate and compare.

**D3:** candidates describe, explain, evaluate and compare. Answers of 2–4 pages with roughly 7–15 explained points (blog).

**Command verbs** (WSET Theory Guidance):
- describe: precise detail
- explain: because → therefore
- compare: similarities and differences
- evaluate: describe → explain → pros → cons → judgement

**Tasting:** flights of 3 wines (same grape, same country, same region, unrelated); 100 marks per flight. Per wine:
- tasting note: appearance 2, nose 6, palate 10 (12 for reds, including tannin nature)
- quality: 6 (1 for the level, 5 for reasons)
- ageing: 3 (1 for suitable or not, 2 for reasons). "Suitable" means the wine will improve, not just hold.

Identification is marked holistically on reasons: varietal markers, climate signals, winemaking, style range, quality range,
bottle age. A wrong answer still earns marks for sound evidence. Weak answers repeat the tasting note instead of arguing
from it. Diploma wines are clean; faults are examined in D1 theory.

## 5. How candidates study; the gap only a simulation fills
- **Methods:** pre-reading, flashcards, command-verb parsing, explaining aloud, timed mocks, examiner reports, study and
  tasting groups.
- **Tools:** Anki, Quizlet, Brainscape, Sommo, GuildSomm, Wine Folly, Wine Scholar Guild, WSET's online classroom.
- **The gap:** none of these tools simulate production or teach cause and effect; they are recall tools. The game uniquely
  offers:
  - a "because" on every descriptor
  - controlled counterfactuals: same site and vintage, one decision changed
  - real vintage weather as evidence
  - immediate, calibrated feedback where a self-studier has no tutor

## 6. A question bank the game can generate (offline, deterministic, no language model)
1. **MCQ** from rulebooks and variety tables; distractors are same-category siblings.
2. **Label reading** of simulated Wines ("why can't this be Kabinett?").
3. **Ranking and comparison** (extends the vintage quiz): "rank by tannin and explain the highest".
4. **Chain builder:** assemble factor → mechanism → effect → style from tiles with decoys. Marked exactly against the
   sheet's why-links, in 2–7-mark parts. Only generated where the sim reproduces the vintage.
5. **Free-text short answer with a rubric.**
   - A point needs the factor term and the effect term in the same sentence, joined by a linking word.
   - Contradictions subtract; the score is capped at the part's marks.
   - The model chain and a self-marking checklist are always shown.
6. **Describe the style** against the median sheet of a simulated style.
7. **Explain the difference** between two Wines that differ in one decision. The Diploma version adds "evaluate
   premium vs inexpensive".
8. **Quality-argument builder:** a level, positive and negative evidence with decoys, and "why not higher". The Diploma
   version marks 1 + 5 for quality and 1 + 2 for ageing.
9. **Identification with reasons** on themed flights, marked on the evidence categories.

Show an "exam-style" percentage with the public thresholds, labelled unofficial.

## 7. Roadmap
**Level 3, top 10:**
1. **Conformance pass on the sheet (S–M):**
   - readiness with four options; brown colours; other observations
   - split white fruit and add pepper; credit "simple"; ripeness qualifiers; separate palate flavours
   - absolute climate class beside the relative one; fix the doc drift
2. **Quality argument, not a count (M):** positive and negative evidence, "why not higher"; recalibrate the high skew.
3. **Chain-builder short-answer items from the why-links (M).**
4. **Rosé and carbonic / semi-carbonic maceration** as cellar options (S–M).
5. **Eiswein and Süssreserve in the Mosel, drying as information (M).**
6. **Regions reusing the existing grapes (M each):** Chablis/Mâconnais, Rheingau/Pfalz, Sonoma/Willamette,
   Marlborough/Central Otago Pinot, Coonawarra/Margaret River Cabernet.
7. **Law and label MCQ bank as data (M).**
8. **New grapes (L):** Sauvignon Blanc, Merlot, Syrah, Grenache; then Chenin, Sangiovese, Nebbiolo, Tempranillo.
9. **Service desk (S–M):** storage, serving, decanting, food pairing, customer scenarios; high-SO₂ and out-of-condition
   faults.
10. **Sparkling and fortified primer:** traditional-method sparkling simulated (L); tank, Asti, Port, Sherry, Muscat as
    cards (S).

**Diploma, top 5:**
1. **Diploma tasting mode (M):** texture, improve-vs-hold ageing, 7-indicator quality argument, 2/6/10–12/6/3 marks.
2. **Themed blind flights with identification and reasons (M).**
3. **Evaluate/compare essay trainer from counterfactual runs (L).**
4. **Deeper D1 techniques (L):** flash détente, micro-oxygenation, smoke taint, precision viticulture.
5. **Vintage and climate-trend analytics 1990–2025 per Region as an essay evidence bank (M).**
