# Research track B — Fun and gamification without losing the simulator feel

Part of the 2026-09-30 fun-and-learning research (synthesis: `2026-09-30-fun-and-learning.md`). Web research by a research
agent; sources were checked to resolve. Practitioner frameworks (Octalysis, most design talks) are not empirical studies.
Tags: **[fun · sim-vibe risk · effort · learning synergy]**.

**Bottom line.** For two adults who study on purpose, what holds them is not points: it is competence, autonomy and doing it
together. The mechanics worth taking make anticipation, reveal and comparison the core loop, and that loop is also retrieval
practice. The biggest risk is a generic XP/badge layer: expected tangible rewards undermine free-choice motivation while
informational feedback raises it (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999), and a badges-and-leaderboard course ended with lower
motivation and lower exam scores (Hanus & Fox 2015).

## 1. What makes management sims fun while staying serious
- Fun is learning patterns; boredom comes when the pattern is mastered or unseeable (Koster). 1990–2025 weather × three
  Regions is a big pattern space: surface vintage character and cause→effect instead of hiding it behind sliders.
- An interesting decision has no dominant answer (Meier, GDC 2012). Watch for "always High Programme".
- Hundred Days previews slider outcomes on a stat sheet: easy to learn, called repetitive; its loved moment is naming and
  labelling the wine. Terroir (2017) runs on randomness tension plus annual awards.
- PowerWash Simulator: realism where the skill lives (8/10), simplified elsewhere (4/10); every finished element is
  acknowledged. Here: winemaking cause→effect is the 8/10; economics and admin the 4/10.
- Football Manager: newcomers quit in the first hour; FM26 answered with layered detail and an in-game encyclopedia that
  links to the screens.
- Papers, Please introduces rules day by day; maps onto a curriculum. Stardew bundles: many small rewards, each completed
  room unlocks a capability. Cozy games: safety and softness, contrast deepens it ("cozy cellar, tense sky").

Ideas: (1) predict-then-reveal with confidence on your own Wines **[H·L·S·yes]**; (2) staged reveals: must → barrel sample
3 and 9 months → bottling **[H·L·M·yes]**; (3) style bundles in an Estate book (Kabinett, Spätlese, whole-bunch Pinot…)
**[M–H·L·M·yes]**; (4) the Mentor's one-concept-per-Vintage curriculum, letters after retirement **[M·L·S·yes]**;
(5) Terroirpedia codex linked to science notes and screens **[M·L·M·yes]**; (6) restrained juice on 3D tasks **[M·M·M·no]**.

## 2. Gamification frameworks and evidence
- SDT/PENS: autonomy, competence and relatedness each predict enjoyment and future play (Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006).
- Sailer et al. 2017: badges, leaderboards and performance graphs raised competence; story and teammates raised
  relatedness; no element raised decision-freedom autonomy. "Gamification is not effective per se." Hamari et al. 2014:
  positive but context-dependent.
- Serious games beat conventional instruction on learning (d = 0.29) and retention (d = 0.36) but not motivation; best
  combined with other instruction over multiple sessions (Wouters et al. 2013): the game and the WSET books work together.
- Critiques: pointsification (Robertson), "exploitationware" (Bogost); RECIPE (Nicholson) avoids rewards for long-term
  change; Octalysis white-hat vs black-hat drives (practitioner). MDA: players meet aesthetics first.
- Streaks: an intact streak raises engagement, a broken one lowers it, a repair option softens it (Silverman & Barasch 2023).

Ideas: (7) competence graphs instead of XP totals **[M·L·S·yes]**; (8) discovery codex entries on first frost night, first
botrytis, first stuck ferment **[M·L·S·yes]**; (9) a gentler streak: weekly target, repairable, no shaming **[L·L·S·yes]**.

## 3. Scenario and challenge design
- Two Point Hospital: one star to progress, two and three stars optional mastery. MSFS presets from All Assists to True to
  Life; DCS Flaming Cliffs simplifies cockpits but keeps the flight model: simplify the interface, never the model. Into the
  Breach: telegraphed information makes failure feel owned. KerbalEdu added data tools and a history campaign.

Ideas: (10) famous-vintage scenarios with three medals and a cited "what real growers did" debrief; goals are winemaking
outcomes, never profit **[H·L·M·yes]**; (11) blind-vintage mode: play a hidden real year, revealed at bottling
**[H·L·S·yes]**; (12) assist levels that change information, not rules (Guided / Standard / True to life) **[M·L·M·yes]**;
(13) ironman flag, no rewind after the weather is revealed **[M·L·S·no]**.

## 4. Narrative and emergent story
- Simulated stories only land when players can perceive the causes (Sylvester). RimWorld paces tension with named
  storytellers and weaves colony history into generated text. Frostpunk's end timelapse works; unfair verdicts backfire.

Ideas: (14) "Vintage in review" at bottling: timelapse plus 6–8 cause cards linked to the descriptors they caused,
descriptive not moralising **[H·L·M·yes]**; (15) Estate chronicle with both players' notes **[M·L·S–M·yes]**; (16) staff
with defensible, cited schools of thought (whole bunch vs destem) **[M·M·M·yes]**; (17) a neighbour Estate with a
contrasting philosophy at your Wine Fair **[M·M·L·yes]**.

## 5. Two-player / couple play
- Gaming hurts couples mainly when only one plays; shared play can raise satisfaction (Ahlstrom et al. 2012). Wordle: same
  puzzle, spoiler-free comparison. Committing an answer before a joint reveal applies retrieval practice (Roediger &
  Karpicke 2006) and productive failure (Richland et al. 2009). A two-person leaderboard always makes one person lose.

Ideas: (18) parallel Vintages from a seed code, then pour each other's Wine blind and diff the decisions **[H·L·M·yes]**;
(19) Tasting duet: same flight, locked answers, side-by-side reveal, disagreement hotspots, a team score **[H·L·S–M·yes]**;
(20) ghost overlay of the partner's decisions and ripening curve **[M·L·S–M·yes]**; (21) shared Estate with roles and a
written fruit report at harvest hand-off **[M·L·L·yes]**.

## 7. Keeping the simulator feel
- FS22 Precision Farming makes knowledge earned (sample, lab, map). Assists keep realism optional, not diluted. Real data,
  units, instruments and logbooks are credibility cues; real 1990–2025 weather is the strongest one.

Ideas: (26) instruments as interface: refractometer in the Region's unit, hydrometer, pH meter, printed lab report
**[M·L·M·yes]**; (27) a dated cellar log **[M·L·S·yes]**; (28) provenance badges on data **[L·L·S·yes]**.

## 8. Sensory substitutes and taste-along
- Expertise is perceptual more than verbal (Parr, Heatherbell & White 2002): a game cannot train the nose; it can train
  reasoning and vocabulary and organise real tasting. Colour dominates (Morrot et al. 2001: white dyed red described as red).
  Music shifts perceived acidity (Wang & Spence 2015); price shifts pleasantness (Plassmann et al. 2008). The aroma wheel
  (Noble et al. 1987) and Le Nez du Vin kits are existing tools.

Ideas: (29) taste-along with a real bottle, both partners mark the real glass, compare with the sim's profile
**[H·L·M·yes]**; (30) aroma-kit prompts ("sniff vial #29") **[M·L·S·yes]**; (31) accurate colour, a black-glass option,
labels hidden in blind modes **[M·L·S·yes]**; (32) a quiet-room hint and a codex page on crossmodal bias **[L·L·S·yes]**.

15 own Wine at a show (after calibration). Just below: style bundles, assist levels, instruments, food pairing.

## Anti-patterns
Profit as the score; XP or currency for everything and loot-style rewards; outcome previews on sliders; head-to-head
leaderboards for two; punitive streaks and nag notifications; narrative verdicts not grounded in sim causes; dominant
strategies; hints that give the answer away; rewinds in scored modes; confetti on serious decisions; precise numbers the
calibration does not back; copying WSET wording; implying the game trains the nose.
