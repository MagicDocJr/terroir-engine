# Regions and Plots

Scope: the three MVP Regions, and the 12 fictional **Plots** with their **Blocks** (spec §4.2).
- **Machine-readable source of truth:** `data/plots.json`, generated by `tools/gen_plots.py`. The tables in §3 are regenerated from the same data by that script; don't hand-edit them.
- **Naming:** Region names are real; Plot names are fictional. Each Plot sits at a **real representative location** (lat, lon, elevation), so Historical weather can be looked up (ERA5 via Open-Meteo, spec §3.4). The coordinates are *representative of the Plot type* and do not identify any real vineyard.

**Game simplifications (labelled in-game):**
- Land is sold **bare and plantable**, and the purchase includes any planting authorisation (see `law.md`).
- Prices are EUR at 2026 levels in every Timeline year.

## 1. Region summaries

### Mosel (Germany, EU zone A)
- **Climate:** cool continental with river moderation.
  - Annual mean 9.7 °C; growing-season mean 14.1 °C.
  - **760 mm** rain per year, **60 %** of it in the growing season.
  - Direct solar radiation in the growing season ~652 kWh/m², highest on the steep slopes.
  - [MOSEL-PS §9.2]
- **Topography:** vineyards at 65–375 m, average 180 m. Mostly SE–S–SW facing, average aspect 195° (SSW) [MOSEL-PS §9.1]. About **3,000 ha of steep slopes** out of **8,440 ha** planted, the world's largest steep-slope wine region [MOSEL-DATEN].
- **Soils:** in the Middle Mosel, **Devonian rocks** dominate (quartzitic sandstone, quartzite, clay slate), in an incised valley of the Rhenish Slate Mountains. The Upper Mosel has Muschelkalk and Keuper limestone [MOSEL-PS §9.1]. Slate soils **warm quickly** [MOSEL-PS §9.2].
- **Hazards:**
  - **Autumn valley fog:** cold air from the Hunsrück and Eifel meets the warm river. The alternation of damp morning fog and afternoon sun **favours noble rot** [MOSEL-PS §9.2].
  - **Floods on flat alluvium:** these areas are normally excluded from the appellation [MOSEL-PS §9.2].
  - **Spring frost** in low, flat sites (`UNVERIFIED` frequency; comes from the weather data).
  - **Downy mildew** in wet years (see `vintage-notes.md`, 2021).
  - **Labour:** steep slopes limit mechanisation, so vineyards need high labour input [MOSEL-PS §9.3].
- **Style anchors:** late ripening; cool temperatures slow acid loss, giving a marked, precise acid structure; moderate sugar even at full ripeness, giving filigree wines with moderate alcohol [MOSEL-PS §9.4.1].

### Côte d'Or (Burgundy, France, EU zone C I, `UNVERIFIED`)
- **Climate:** semi-continental. `UNVERIFIED`: an annual mean of about 11 °C and about 700–800 mm of rain, spread through the year with thunderstorm peaks in summer. The simulation uses ERA5 data for Beaune, so these numbers are only for orientation.
- **Topography:** a narrow east/south-east-facing escarpment. Slopes are mostly gentle, about 3–15 %, at about 220–400 m, with the **Hautes-Côtes** plateau behind at about 350–450 m (`UNVERIFIED` ranges). The flat land east of the slope (towards the Saône plain) is regional-appellation land.
- **Soils:** `UNVERIFIED` (standard reference, cited from memory): **Middle Jurassic limestones and marls** on the slope. Brown calcareous soils; stony colluvium mid-slope; deeper clay and alluvium on the flats. **Active lime** is high in places, a **lime-induced chlorosis** risk that makes rootstock choice matter (see the viticulture docs).
- **Hazards:**
  - **spring frost** (2016, 2021)
  - **hail** (backlog: not derivable from weather data, spec §4.5)
  - **heat waves** (2003, 2019–2020)
  - grey rot in wet Septembers
- **Rules that shape viticulture:** high planting density (village and 1er Cru ≥ 9,000 vines/ha), a bud cap, **no irrigation**, and yield caps (`law.md` §3).

### Napa Valley (California, USA)
- **Climate:** Mediterranean. `UNVERIFIED`: winter rain, about 600–900 mm on the valley floor and more on the mountains, falling mostly November–March; dry summers. There's a strong **south-to-north warming gradient**: Carneros is cooled by fog and wind from San Pablo Bay; up-valley is warmer. Mountain sites sit **above the summer fog line**.
- **Frost:** valley-floor frost in March–May is managed with wind machines. The UC Davis cost study budgets 12 wind-machine hours per season (March–May) and notes recent springs have been less severe [UCD-NAPA-2020].
- **Pests and diseases:** powdery mildew is the main disease. There's growing pressure from **European grapevine moth** and **vine mealybug**, and Pierce's disease occurs [UCD-NAPA-2020].
- **Hazards:**
  - **wildfire smoke** (2017, 2020; backlog as an event, spec §4.5)
  - **heat spikes** (e.g. Labor Day 2017; see vintage notes)
  - **drought**
  - frost on the valley floor
- **Development rules:** slopes ≥ 5 % need county approval (an erosion control plan) [UCD-NAPA-2020]. The valley is "effectively planted out" [UCD-NAPA-2020].
- **Soils:** `UNVERIFIED`: very diverse. Heavy loams and clays on the valley floor; gravelly **alluvial fans ("benches")** at the foot of the western hills (e.g. Rutherford); shallow **clay over claypan** in Carneros; **thin volcanic and sedimentary** soils in the mountains.

## 2. How Block soil values are derived

- **TAW (total available water, mm)** = rooting depth [m] × (1 − stones fraction) × **AWC(texture)** [mm per m].
- **AWC** is taken as the mid-point of the FAO-56 texture ranges (field capacity minus wilting point) [FAO-56 Table 19, cited from memory]:

| Texture | AWC (mm/m) |
|---|---|
| sand | 80 |
| loamy sand | 90 |
| sandy loam | 130 |
| loam | 155 |
| silt loam | 160 |
| clay loam | 155 |
| silty clay | 160 |
| clay | 160 |
| gravelly loam (fine earth) | 150 |
| slate loam (fine earth) | 140 |

- **Slate and volcanic rock caveat:** vine roots follow **fractures** into weathered rock well below the fine-earth "rooting depth". A plain TAW calculation therefore **underestimates** the water actually available on slate slopes; e.g. the "Rocky rib" Block computes only 24 mm. `UNVERIFIED`: the water-balance model should add a **fracture-water reserve** for Blocks with ≥ 50 % stones on slate or volcanic parent rock. It should be calibrated so that steep Mosel slate vines show moderate, not lethal, stress in dry years such as 2018.
- **Microclimate indices** (0–1) are *relative* modifiers the simulation applies to ERA5 data:
  - **frostPocket:** extra night-time cooling on clear, calm nights
  - **fogFrequency:** extra leaf and bunch wetness hours
  - **windExposure:** faster drying, less disease, slower ripening, thicker skins
  - **radiativeHeat:** a warmer canopy from rock and wall re-radiation, with sunburn risk

  The magnitudes of each modifier are defined in the climate docs (another milestone-1 file) and are `UNVERIFIED` here.
- **All per-Block numbers are design values** chosen to be typical of the soil types named (`UNVERIFIED` per Block). They make the within-Plot contrasts (terroir) teachable; they don't describe real parcels.
- **Starting capital (game value):** **€600,000**. With it the player can afford any cheap or mid-low Plot plus Planting, Establishment and the first vintage, and can just reach the mid-high Plots. The expensive Côte d'Or and Napa Plots are the long-term goal (see `economy.md`).

## 4. Design notes (why these Plots)

- **Mosel:**
  - The cheap flat alluvium teaches **frost pockets, fog and botrytis**, and why valley floors rarely make great Riesling.
  - The steep terraces teach **heat accumulation on slate** and the **labour cost of steep slopes**: hand work only, 1,200+ labour-hours per ha (see `economy.md`).
  - In the Mosel the land is cheap; the labour is what's expensive.
- **Côte d'Or:**
  - **Classification** caps the Legal category, so the same Pinot Noir grown on the flats can only ever be "Bourgogne".
  - The Hautes-Côtes plateau adds **altitude, wind and chlorosis** (a lime-tolerant rootstock choice).
  - Prices climb steeply with Classification. SAFER 2024 data put the Côte de Beaune average at about €1.05 M/ha for village whites and above €2.5 M/ha for Premier Cru whites [SAFER-2024 via REUSSIR].
- **Napa:**
  - **Carneros vs. the bench** is the cool/warm contrast inside one Region.
  - The mountain Plot teaches **low-yield, high-tannin** fruit and **hillside development rules**.
  - Bench and valley-floor prices use the **plantable-land** values in the UC Davis 2020 study (prime US$200–250k per acre; secondary US$50–240k per acre) [UCD-NAPA-2020].
