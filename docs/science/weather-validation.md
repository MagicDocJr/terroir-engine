# Historical weather: validation against climatology and known events

Data: `data/weather/*.csv.gz` (Open-Meteo `era5_seamless`, 1990–2025; see `data/weather/README.md`). Indices come from `tools/weather/validate.py`, and the per-year appendix below is generated by that script.

## Verdict
- **Timing and ranking of years are reliable in all three Regions.** The hot years, cool years, frost nights, heatwaves and rain events land on the right dates, and the warmest, coolest and driest years match the history.
- **Absolute levels need correction in two places before simulating:**
  1. **Napa temperatures run far too warm**: about +510 °C·days against the published Oakville figure.
  2. **Radiative spring frost and heavy rain in hilly terrain are underestimated.** Frost minima are 5–6 °C too mild; the Napa October 2011 storm comes in about 75% short.
- Mosel and Côte d'Or levels are plausible but **not yet checked against a published station normal** (`UNVERIFIED`).

## Method
- **Winkler GDD:** Σ max(0, (Tmax+Tmin)/2 − 10) over 1 Apr–31 Oct (Amerine & Winkler 1944). Regions in °C·days: I ≤ 1389, II ≤ 1667, III ≤ 1944, IV ≤ 2222, V above.
- **Huglin HI:** Σ max(0, ((Tmean−10)+(Tmax−10))/2) × k over 1 Apr–30 Sep, where k = 1.06 (Mosel, 49.9° N), 1.05 (Côte d'Or, 47.0° N) and 1.00 (Napa, 38.4° N) (Huglin 1978). Classes follow Tonietto & Carbonneau 2004.
- **Frost:** Tmin ≤ 0 °C; "hard frost" is Tmin ≤ −2 °C. Heat thresholds are Tmax ≥ 30 °C and ≥ 35 °C.
- **Normals** use 1991–2020 (the WMO standard period).

Napa: part of the gap is real warming. The AVA rule's figures predate 1993, and Napa Valley maximum temperatures rose about 1.4 °F from the 1940s to the 2010s (San Francisco Chronicle, citing station records). That explains at most about +100–150 °C·d. The rest (about +350 °C·d) is reanalysis bias: a coarse cell that can't resolve the San Pablo Bay marine layer and fog. Mosel/Côte d'Or: the Region I / I–II labels are the common description, but no primary source with station numbers was retrieved in this pass (`UNVERIFIED`).

## Warming trend (Winkler, mean of 1990–1999 → mean of 2016–2025)
Mosel **+252** °C·d (1117 → 1368) · Côte d'Or **+305** (1288 → 1593) · Napa **+160** (2236 → 2395). This is the climate-change lesson the Timeline is meant to teach (ADR 0002). A 2020s Mosel season now carries the heat a 1990s Côte d'Or season had.

## What the simulation must do with this data
1. **Correct the Napa bias** at Region level before any Block adjustment. Scale Tmax/Tmin so the 1991–2020 Winkler normal lands in Region III (target ≈ 1,850 °C·d: the published value plus documented warming, `UNVERIFIED` pending a station normal). Keep the day-to-day anomalies so events like 6 Sep 2022 stay extreme. Consider scaling rain by about 1.25.
2. **Add a radiative-frost model for Blocks.** On clear, calm nights (low wind, low humidity, a large day-night temperature range in this data), frost-pocket and valley-floor Blocks cool several °C below the grid Tmin. Calibrate it so 7 April 2021 reaches about −5 to −7 °C in the Côte d'Or frost-pocket Plot.
3. **Heavy-rain events:** consider amplifying single-day totals above a Region-specific percentile (the data gets the timing right but compresses the peaks). Useful for harvest-rain and botrytis scenarios.
4. **Elevation and aspect:** the grid cells sit at valley-floor elevation. Every Block applies the lapse rate from the cell elevation to its own, plus slope and aspect radiation (spec §3.4).

## Open questions
- **Station normals** (1991–2020) for Bernkastel-Kues/Trier (DWD) and Beaune/Dijon (Météo-France), and a recent Oakville station normal (a CIMIS or NOAA station in Oakville or St Helena), to replace the `UNVERIFIED` entries and set bias-correction targets precisely.
- Should the bias correction happen at data build time (corrected files) or at runtime in the simulation core (raw data plus a documented correction)? **Recommendation:** runtime. The raw data stays traceable to its source, and the correction is testable and cited.
- ERA5-Land's hourly data would allow leaf-wetness hours for the disease models (spec §4.5), but is roughly 24× larger. Decide when the disease models are built.
